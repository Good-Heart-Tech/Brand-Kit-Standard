# Voice and tone

We sound like a friendly barista who really knows coffee: warm, curious, and
happy to help, never talking down to anyone. Our voice stays the same
everywhere. Our tone shifts with the moment.

| Situation | Tone | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Product pages | Inviting, concrete | "Bright and juicy, with notes of red apple and brown sugar. Great as pour-over or drip." |
| Cafe signage and menu boards | Short, friendly | "Today's drip: Switchback. Ask us about refills." |
| Email to shoppers | Warm, useful | "Your Fresh Drop roasted Tuesday and ships tomorrow. Here is a brew tip for this one." |
| Social media | Playful, local | "Fresh batch of Summit just came off the roaster. The North End smells amazing right now." |
| Wholesale pitches | Professional, specific | "We roast to order every week, deliver on a set day, and train your staff on dial-in at no extra cost." |
| Support replies | Patient, owning the problem | "Sorry your bag arrived late. We are sending a fresh one today, and you do not need to send anything back." |
| Press | Factual, quotable | Lead with what is new, then one sentence on who we are. |
| Legal and policy | Formal, plain | No jokes. Short sentences. |

## Always

- Explain coffee terms the first time ("natural process, where the fruit dries
  on the bean")
- Lead with how it tastes and how to brew it, not with scores or jargon
- Put the roast date and the practical details (size, grind, price) where
  people can find them fast
- Say sorry plainly when we get it wrong, then say what we are doing about it

## Never

- Make anyone feel silly for how they drink their coffee
- Rush people in support replies or ask them to prove a problem
- Ask for payment, gift cards, or bank changes by email or text (see
  `security/brand-protection.md`)
