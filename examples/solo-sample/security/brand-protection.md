# Protecting the Juniper Lane Studio brand

I am a business of one, so this list is short on purpose. The real risks are
someone taking over my accounts, a fake invoice reaching a client, and a fake
profile copying my portfolio. Check items off as you go, and review the list
once a year.

## Accounts

- [ ] Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is on for my email, my portfolio
      website, and every payment tool (invoicing, card payments, bank)
- [ ] MFA is also on for my social media accounts
- [ ] Passwords live in a password manager, and none are reused
- [ ] Recovery codes are saved somewhere safe that is not my email

## Invoices and payments

- [ ] Every invoice comes from one invoicing tool, never as an attached PDF
      with bank details typed in by hand
- [ ] Every proposal and welcome email tells clients: "My payment details will
      never change by email. If you get a message saying they did, it is not
      from me. Check with me on my website first."
- [ ] I never ask clients or vendors to pay by gift card or wire transfer

## Email

- [ ] SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are set up for my email domain (my email provider
      or an IT volunteer can help)
- [ ] DMARC is set to "reject" so fake emails using my domain get blocked

## Fake profiles and copied work

- [ ] A few times a year I search my studio name and run a reverse image
      search on my best portfolio photos
- [ ] I know where each social platform's impersonation report form is
- [ ] My website lists my real social profiles, so clients can check

## If someone impersonates me

1. Do not reply to or click anything in the fake message.
2. Tell affected clients directly, by phone or in person, and remind them that
   my payment details never change by email.
3. Report fake social profiles through the platform's impersonation form.
4. Report fake websites to Google Safe Browsing and the domain's registrar.
5. Ask the person reporting it to use my contact page:
   https://juniperlane.example/contact

## What I share, and why it is safe

Printers, vendors, and clients get my logos, colors, the CSS color file, and the
logo rules. These are already visible on my website, so sharing them adds no
real risk. I do **not** share proposal or invoice designs, pricing, or my email
signature, because those are what a scammer would copy to send a fake invoice.
